🎯 Top stories of the day

International 🌍

  1. Jaishankar's 'serial practitioner of terrorism' attack on Pakistan at UNGA81 — delivering India's national statement at the 81st UNGA general debate on 26 Sept, EAM S. Jaishankar said "yesterday, a serial practitioner of terrorism misrepresented facts before this Assembly. Arguments were invoked to normalise terrorism and claim immunity from its consequences. That will not stand." He asserted India's right to defend itself against terrorism "will be exercised", that "friendship and goodwill cannot co-exist with terrorism", and reaffirmed zero tolerance — the financial aspects of terrorism "should be exposed and countered". The remarks followed Pakistan PM Shehbaz Sharif's address a day earlier, where Sharif credited US President Donald Trump with a "timely intervention" in the May 2025 four-day confrontation and criticised India over the suspension of the Indus Waters Treaty; India had exercised its right of reply through diplomat Petal Gehlot. 🎓⭐
  2. Trump–Xi White House summit ends with no breakthrough — US President Donald Trump hosted Chinese President Xi Jinping on 24 Sept (Xi's first US visit in over a decade): warmer tone but no breakthroughs on Taiwan, AI, tariffs or the Iran war. The two sides agreed to a two-month extension of the US–China trade truce to 10 January 2027 (it was due to expire in November), continue work on a Board of Trade for limited goods, and hold further talks on AI — Xi said AI must be "always under human control" and invoked the Thucydides Trap (competition, not confrontation). Rare-earth/magnet supply commitments, soybean purchases and the 200-Boeing-aircraft deal stayed unresolved; the only headline "gift" was two giant pandas for Atlanta Zoo. 🎓⭐
  3. Macron renews push to regulate UNSC veto at UNGA81 — French President Emmanuel Macron renewed the French-Mexican Initiative (floated 2013, launched 2015) calling on the P5 to voluntarily refrain from using the veto in cases of genocide, crimes against humanity and large-scale war crimes. Backing has reached 128 UN Member States (21 joined in mid-2026) — the first time two permanent members (France and the UK) formally committed. The veto is not named in the UN Charter — it flows from Article 27(3) (affirmative vote of 9 including concurring P5 votes); related mechanisms: the Liechtenstein Initiative (UNGA Resolution 76/262, 2022) forcing a UNGA meeting within 10 working days of any veto, and the ACT Code of Conduct. Any charter amendment to abolish the veto needs two-thirds of UNGA plus ratification by two-thirds of members including all five permanent members (Article 108) — the amendment paradox. 🎓

National Security & Governance 🏛️

  1. AFSPA extended for 6 months in parts of Manipur, Nagaland, Arunachal — in Gazette notifications issued 25 Sept, the MHA extended the Armed Forces (Special Powers) Act, 1958 in specified areas of the three states for six months from 1 October 2026 (till 31 March 2027), following a review of law and order. In Manipur, the disturbed-area declaration covers the whole state except areas under 13 police stations in five districts (Imphal West, Imphal East, Thoubal, Bishnupur, Kakching). In Nagaland, AFSPA continues in 9 districts (Dimapur, Niuland, Chumoukedima, Mon, Kiphire, Noklak, Phek, Peren, Meluri) plus areas under 21 police stations in Kohima, Mokokchung, Longleng, Wokha and Zunheboto. In Arunachal Pradesh, it covers Tirap, Changlang and Longding districts plus Namsai, Mahadevpur and Chowkham police-station areas in Namsai. Section 3 empowers the Centre to declare a "disturbed area". 🎓⭐
  2. The RUPP conundrum: Ashok Lavasa flags shell-party funding — former Election Commissioner Ashok Lavasa flagged unaccounted political funding after a BBC investigation into suspicious donations to Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPPs — parties registered under Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951, without national/state-party status). ADR figures cited: RUPP income rose 223% in FY 2022–23, while only 739 of 2,764 parties filed audited accounts; the ECI delisted 334 of 2,854 RUPPs in August 2025 for prolonged electoral inactivity (delisting ≠ statutory deregistration — a 2002 SC ruling permits deregistration only in exceptional cases); Section 13A gives conditional tax exemptions to registered parties; estimated revenue forgone through tax exemptions on political contributions: ₹11,813 crore over a decade. Reform calls: CAG-empanelled audits, statutory deregistration powers, a party-expenditure ceiling, and a unified digital financial-reporting portal. 🎓⭐
  3. Delhi HC: Muslim personal-law marriage no shield from POCSO/BNS for sex with wife under 18 — the Delhi High Court ruled that a Muslim personal-law marriage does not protect a man from prosecution under the POCSO Act or the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for sexual acts with a wife below 18 years of age, and refused to quash the case — an important exam-ready junction of personal law and criminal law. 🎓
  4. Operation Drishti: AFMS performs 220 sight-restoring surgeries at Tangdhar — Armed Forces Medical Services surgeons performed 220 sight-restoring surgeries during the two-day 10th Mega Eye Surgical Camp at an underground surgical centre in Tangdhar, Kupwara, along the LoC. 🎓

Economy & Infrastructure 🏦🎓

  1. Centre notifies India's first four 'Mega Ports' under the Indian Ports Act, 2025 — the Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways notified Deendayal Port (Kandla), Jawaharlal Nehru Port (Nhava Sheva), Paradip Port and Mundra Port as Mega Ports on 25 Sept (notification in force on publication), the first set under the new classification introduced by Section 73 of the Indian Ports Act, 2025. Criteria (notified July 2026): ≥150 MMT annual cargo for bulk ports or ≥7.5M TEU for container ports; the classification stays valid 5 years even if throughput later falls below threshold, then is reviewed; it does not alter statutory status — Mundra (Adani-operated) remains a "port other than a major port". Paradip is the only east-coast Mega Port. 🎓⭐
  2. SWIFT payment system in focus as BRICS eyes alternatives — the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication (SWIFT) is a member-owned cooperative founded in 1973, HQ'd in La Hulpe, Belgium, supervised collectively by the G10 central banks — a pure secure messaging network (not a settlement bank) linking 11,000+ banking and securities organisations across 200+ countries via BIC/ISO 20022 codes. Alternatives gaining traction amid sanctions weaponisation of the dollar: CIPS (China, 2015, yuan internationalisation), SPFS (Russia, 2014), and the blockchain-based Project mBridge multi-CBDC platform. 🏦🎓

Food & Consumer Regulation 🥛

  1. FSSAI moves to bar 'analogue paneer' from using the name paneer — the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India proposed a draft amendment to the Food Safety and Standards (Prohibition and Restrictions on Sales) Regulations, 2011 to prohibit manufacture, marketing and sale of non-dairy analogue products under the name "paneer". Analogue paneer replaces milk fat/protein with vegetable oils, starches and plant proteins — 50–70% cheaper to make than real paneer — and must be sold as a dairy analogue with transparent labelling; hotels, caterers and food-delivery platforms would have to disclose its use. 🎓

Science & Environment 🔬🌱

  1. Typhoon Dujuan batters Tokyo, eastern Japan — Japan's 25th named storm of the season (name contributed by China, means the azalea flower) dumped a record 832 mm of rain in 48 hours on Izu Oshima, with sustained winds of 130 km/h and gusts of 194 km/h; it triggered all-time high rainfall in Tokyo's Edogawa ward, disrupted Haneda/Narita flights and bullet-train services, and its interaction with Japan's stationary autumn rain front (Akisame zensen) intensified flooding. 🎓

Sports 🏆

  1. Asian Games 2026, Day 7: kabaddi double gold takes India to 30 medals — on 26 Sept India completed a golden kabaddi sweep over Iran in Aichi-Nagoya: the men won 40-34 (trailing 18-17 at half-time; Arjun Deshwal's raiding and Sumit's defending turned the final) and the women edged Iran 37-34 earlier in the day. India's 9th men's and 4th women's Asian Games kabaddi golds took the Day-7 haul to 7 medals (2 gold, 3 silver, 2 bronze) and the overall tally to 30: 4 gold, 12 silver, 14 bronze. Other 26 Sept medals: Sawan Barwal — marathon silver in 2:11:37 (national record), India's first Asian Games marathon medal since 1982 (44 years); Tajinderpal Singh Toor — men's shot put silver behind Iran's Mohammadreza Tayebi's Games Record 20.81m; women's 50m rifle 3-position team (Tilottama Sen, Vidarsa K. Vinod, Ashi Chouksey) — silver; Prachi Choudhary and the Joshna Chinappa–Velavan Senthilkumar squash pair — bronze; Anahat Singh and Abhay Singh reached the squash singles finals (medals assured). 🎓⭐

Important Days 📅

  1. World Tourism Day — 27 September — the 2026 theme is "Digital Agenda and Artificial Intelligence to Redesign Tourism" and the official global celebration is hosted by El Salvador (San Salvador). World Tourism Day has been observed since 1980 (46th edition), instituted at the 1979 UN Tourism General Assembly in Torremolinos; the date commemorates the adoption of the UN Tourism Statutes on 27 Sept 1970. India's celebration at Bharat Mandapam: tourism contributes 5.22% to GDP and supported 8.46 crore jobs in 2023-24; Domestic Tourist Visits hit 428.55 crore in 2025, Foreign Tourist Visits rose to 2.53 crore, and foreign exchange earnings from tourism reached ₹2,76,831 crore. Digital pillars: e-Visa, the Incredible India Digital Platform and NIDHI+ (National Integrated Database of Hospitality Industry); flagship schemes: Swadesh Darshan 2.0 and PRASHAD. Globally, travel & tourism contributed US$10.9 trillion (~10% of GDP) and 357 million jobs in 2024. 🎓⭐

🌙 Evening updates (27 Sept, evening)

  1. Asian Games, 27 Sept results: India adds 7 medals, tally reaches 37 — India collected four silver and three bronze on 27 Sept at Aichi-Nagoya. Silvers: Ancy Sojan (women's long jump), Abhay Singh (squash men's singles, lost the final to Malaysia's Ng Eain Yow), Anahat Singh (squash women's singles, lost to Malaysia's Sivasangari Subramaniam), and Sarvesh Kushare (men's high jump). Bronzes: Parul Chaudhary (women's 3000m steeplechase), Tejaswin Shankar (decathlon), and Harita Bhadra (women's 200m). The overall tally moved to 37 medals (4 gold, 16 silver, 17 bronze), though India slipped from 10th to 12th in the standings with no new gold; three Indian boxers also confirmed medals by reaching their semifinals. 🎓⭐
  2. ECI announces SIR relief measures — following a full-Commission meeting on 26 Sept, the Election Commission said it would stop physical hearings for unmapped voters or those with logical discrepancies in enumeration forms, that the Form 6 declaration is mandatory only during the ongoing Special Intensive Revision, and that anyone left out may apply to the Electoral Registration Officer. A special enrolment drive for left-out electors starts immediately, Booth Level Officers will visit the houses of voters issued SIR notices, a panel with an independent expert from an IIT/IIIT will review the ECINet platform, and the claims-and-objections period was extended in Delhi and Maharashtra; the revision has been completed in 20 States/UTs. 🎓
  3. 27 UPSC Prelims 2026 questions challenged before CAT — aspirants have challenged 27 questions from the CSE 2026 prelims answer key before the Central Administrative Tribunal: a group of 63 candidates challenged all 27 (a smaller group challenged 8). Disputed questions include one on Grand Slam rules and one on the zero-FIR procedure under the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (where the Hindi and English versions allegedly used different terms). The tribunal is scheduled to hear both petitions on 8 October; several petitioners say they missed the Mains cut-off by a few marks. 🎓
  4. ACC clears bureaucratic reshuffle: new CBSE Chairperson and FSSAI CEO — per ACC orders of 25 Sept, Mandeep K. Bhandari (2001-batch AGMUT IAS) has been appointed Chairperson of the Central Board of Secondary Education, and Rajit Punhani (1991-batch Bihar IAS) has been appointed CEO of the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India; Manisha Sensarma (IES 1995) was named Additional Secretary in the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship. 🎓🏭
  5. India's first LNG-powered train flagged off at Sabarmati — on 27 Sept, Union Home Minister Amit Shah flagged off the country's first LNG (liquefied natural gas)-powered train from Sabarmati Railway Station, Ahmedabad, alongside rail projects worth ₹1,472 crore — including a ₹700-crore WAG-12 locomotive maintenance shed and the Kavach train-protection system on the Sabarmati–Kalol–Sabarmati section (₹48 crore). Two 1,400 HP driving power cars were converted to diesel–LNG dual-fuel, cutting diesel use by ~40%. Railways' diesel consumption fell from 293 crore litres (2015-16) to 108 crore litres (2024-25), with a target of zero by 2030; 99.6% of the network is electrified. 🎓⭐
  6. DRDO signs first high-value TDF deep-tech pact for a quantum refrigerator (announced 24 Sept) — DRDO signed its first high-value deep-tech project agreement under the Technology Development Fund with Alwar-based start-up Zero mK India Pvt Ltd to indigenously develop a 20 millikelvin (mK) dilution refrigerator for quantum applications — the first project under the ₹500-crore TDF corpus for deep-tech. Dilution refrigerators create the ultra-low temperatures quantum hardware needs; the project will be mentored by DRDO's Solid State Physics Laboratory (SSPL), Delhi, and aligns with the National Quantum Mission. 🎓
