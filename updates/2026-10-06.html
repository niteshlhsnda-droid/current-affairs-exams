Nobel Prizes 🌍🔬

  1. Nobel Prize in Medicine 2026: trio honoured for optogenetics — the Nobel Assembly at Sweden's Karolinska Institute announced on 5 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine goes to Karl Deisseroth (54, American, Stanford University), Peter Hegemann (71, German) and Georg Nagel (73, German) for *"discoveries concerning light-gated ion channels and optogenetics"*. The breakthrough: Hegemann and Nagel discovered channelrhodopsin, a light-sensitive protein in the alga *Chlamydomonas* that opens an ion channel when exposed to light; Deisseroth then engineered nerve cells to produce it, so blue light can switch individual nerve cells on or off inside a living brain — the technique, named optogenetics in 2006, is now used in laboratories worldwide to map the neural circuits behind memory, feelings and behaviour, and to study neurological and psychiatric disorders. The three share 12 million Swedish kronor (~US$1.2 million). 🎓⭐

India–Switzerland sign migration and mobility partnership pact — following the wide-ranging talks between PM Narendra Modi and Swiss President Guy Parmelin (day 1 of his 5–7 Oct state visit, New Delhi, 5 October), the two countries signed a migration and mobility partnership pact, decided to expand Swiss market access for Indian exports, and agreed to further deepen cooperation in nuclear energy and defence — with the agenda also covering trade and investment, and critical technologies. The visit marks one year of the India–EFTA TEPA (EFTA = Switzerland, Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein; USD 100 billion investment target over 15 years), and Parmelin participates in the 2nd India–EFTA Prosperity Summit on 7 October. 🎓⭐

🌆 Evening updates

Banking & Economy 🏦

  1. RBI's draft foreign investment rules put AIFs in the spotlight — the draft FEMA (Foreign Investment) Rules, 2026, released by the RBI on 21 July 2026 to replace the NDI Rules, 2019, propose treating Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) with over 50% non-resident interest as indirect foreign investment — closing what SEBI and the RBI see as regulatory arbitrage, where foreign investors use domestically managed AIFs to bypass FDI sectoral caps. Under the NDI Rules, an AIF counted as domestic if its sponsor and investment manager were Indian-owned and controlled; the draft introduces the concept of a foreign-controlled entity (FCE) but lacks AIF-specific ownership/control tests — prompting PE/VC funds to warn it could hurt capital inflows and asking for the status quo. 🏦⭐
  2. SEBI chief warns investors against anonymous tips and unrealistic returns — cautioning retail investors to stay away from anonymous investment tips and promises of unrealistic returns. 🏦
  3. IndiGo raises fuel surcharge on domestic and international flights — the airline introduced a new fuel surcharge of up to ₹6,000 on international routes (with higher charges on domestic flights too) to pass on rising fuel costs. 🏦
  4. Kerala to launch IT Parks Affiliation Scheme today (6 Oct) — the state will launch a scheme to connect IT companies outside major government-owned IT parks with Kerala's state-level technology ecosystem. 🏦🎓
  5. Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026 — the new Act, which recently came into force, replaced the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891. 🏦

Science & Education 🔬

  1. IOAA 2026: India wins 4 gold + 1 silver, finishes joint third — the Indian team won four gold and one silver at the 19th International Olympiad on Astronomy and Astrophysics (IOAA), held in Hanoi, Vietnam, 25 September – 5 October — the largest IOAA ever, with 307 students from 64 countries. Gold medallists: Pranjal Dixit (Hyderabad), Sumant Gupta (Delhi), Akshat Srivastava (Gurugram), Arnav Sehrawat (Faridabad); silver: Harshil Amitkumar Patel (Vadodara). India finished joint third with Vietnam, behind Iran and South Korea (five golds each). The results also complete a historic clean sweep: with all senior Olympiads (Maths, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Astronomy) done, every Indian student this year won gold (16) or silver (8) — a first since 1989. 🎓⭐

🌃 Late-evening updates

Science & Technology 🔬

  1. Nobel Prize in Physics 2026: Francis Halzen for IceCube and 'ghost particles' — the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced on 6 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Physics goes to Francis Halzen (Belgian-born particle physicist, US-based), *"for decisive contributions to the IceCube Neutrino Observatory and the discovery of high-energy neutrinos of astrophysical origin"*. Halzen first proposed a neutrino detector at the South Pole in 1988; the vision became IceCube, which instruments a cubic kilometre of Antarctic ice with light sensors to catch neutrinos — near-massless "ghost particles" that pass through almost everything and travel undeflected from violent cosmic events. IceCube was completed in 2011 and soon detected the first high-energy neutrinos arriving from beyond our solar system, opening a new window on the universe. Prize money: 12 million Swedish kronor (~US$1.2 million / ~₹11.5 crore). This is the second Nobel of 2026 (Medicine on 5 Oct; Chemistry due on 7 Oct). 🎓⭐
