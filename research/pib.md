## How PIB organizes current affairs
- **The primary source**: official press releases, fact sheets, InFocus explainers, research e-books and Budget explainers — straight from every ministry. **100% free, zero upsells, zero ads.**
- **Dozens of releases daily**; a full Hindi section exists.
- **No quizzes, no PDF compilations, no exam framing** — it is raw government communication, not an exam product.
- Cross-exam relevance: best for **UPSC** (schemes, Bills, Mains data points and quotable facts); useful for **PSU/Bank** on regulatory and banking releases (e.g. the PSU-bank appeal, Bankers' Books Evidence Act).

## Strengths
1. **Primary source — zero distortion**: when two prep sites disagree on a scheme's launch date or a Bill's provision, PIB is the tiebreaker.
2. **Deepest per-announcement detail**: full scheme guidelines, Bill texts and data tables no prep site reproduces.
3. **Authoritative Mains material**: quotable government data and scheme specifics lift GS answers.

## Weaknesses
1. **No exam framing** — you must decide what matters; signal is buried in volume (dozens of releases daily).
2. **No exam-ready compilations** — no monthly PDF, no quiz, no one-liners; it's a mine, not a magazine.
3. **Dry and time-expensive** to read raw; inefficient as a daily habit without a filter.

## Notes for exam-prep use
- Best for: **fact-checking every other site's claims** + mining Mains-grade data points. **Cross-check scheme dates, Bill provisions and index numbers against PIB before trusting any prep site.**
- Practical workflow: read a prep site's daily CA (Insights IAS / StudyIQ / Adda247), then use PIB only for items that look exam-critical or contradictory.
- Not for: daily reading; quiz practice; exam-ready revision.
